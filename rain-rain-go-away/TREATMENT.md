# Rain, Rain, Go Away — treatment

Style: Crayon Picture Book (`crayon-book`). 1920×1080, 24 fps, 60 s. Language: English.
Brief: the traditional nursery rhyme (public domain words and tune) sung twice. The melody is carried by an alto recorder as the "singing voice", and the words write themselves onto the page as sing-along captions. There is no spoken or synthetic voice.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One page, two weathers.** The camera never leaves one picture-book page. Verse 1 is the rainy page, verse 2 the same page in the sun, and the change happens on the page itself, under a held wide.
2. **Page turn, before and after.** A rainy page, a page turn, then a sunny page with the same house redrawn.
3. **A page that gets coloured in.** It opens as a nearly colourless page: indigo outlines, grey cloud, blue rain, and the garden only lightly coloured, with drooping flowers. When the sun comes out, it *colours the garden in*. Flowers fill with colour as the children dance past them, the sky gets its blue wash, and the rainbow is drawn on live, arc by arc.

**Chosen: 3, built on the single page of 1.** "Colouring is an action" matches the rhyme's turn (grey to bright), keeps the house and garden in one place throughout, as the brief asks, and gives the film one native move at the peak: the sky-blue wash sweeps in behind the departing cloud and reveals the sun's rays, which were drawn in **white wax** and hidden in the grey sky from the first frame. A page turn (structure 2) would break the "same house and garden" rule and move the turn off the page.

- **Opening (hook in 3 s):** a single blue crayon raindrop draws itself falling down the page and *plops* into a puddle that draws itself in front of the house. More drops follow on the beat.
- **Ending (echo):** the same puddle, now holding a reflection of the rainbow. A butterfly lands on its rim, and its last flutter falls on the final chord.

## 2. Logline and arc

Two small children stuck indoors on a rainy day sing the rain away, and the sun colours their garden in so they can play.

- **Setup (0–25 s):** rain on the house. The children watch through the window, holding a ball and a kite.
- **Turn (25–35 s):** a breeze, drawn as crayon swirls, pushes the grey cloud off the page. The rain stops, there is a breath of silence, then *ding*: the sun pops up and the blue wash reveals its white-wax rays.
- **Ending (35–60 s):** the children run out, play ball, fly the kite and dance. The flowers fill with colour as they pass, butterflies flutter, and the rainbow is drawn on. We settle on the puddle.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Charlie and Lola* (TV, 2005): **learn** the cut-out, substitution-pose acting, child-eye staging, lots of bare paper and short holds on small feelings. **Don't take** the characters, the collage/photo textures or the designs.
- Oliver Jeffers' picture books: **learn** the generous empty paper, one clear action per spread and the gentle humour of small figures against big weather. **Don't take** any characters, lettering or compositions.

## 4. Characters and crayon box

- **Mia**, the older girl (≈2.5 heads tall): round head, two bunches in the brown crayon, a red dress, a yellow kite.
- **Leo**, the younger boy (a head shorter): spiky orange hair, a blue jumper, a red-and-white ball.
- The same model sheet is used in every shot: dot eyes with a white-wax catchlight, pink cheeks, tube limbs, mitten hands.
- **Crayon box (11 + white):** indigo line, grey (cloud), cerulean (rain), red, orange, lemon yellow, leaf green, grass green, pink, violet, brown, and white wax for the hidden sun rays.
- **One watercolour:** a sky-blue wash, for the sky after the turn.

| 1 | 0–10 | Locked full page | Still; the rain draws itself | The page as a stage before anything happens; hook on the first drop |

| 3 | 15–20 | Medium, then up | **Tilt up along their gaze** to the cloud | They want to play; we look where they look |

The signature shot is shot 5: the cloud leaves, there's a breath of silence, the sun pops, and the wash sweeps across and reveals the white-wax rays.

## 8. Captions

These are the book's printed words: Patrick Hand (OFL), 54 px, in indigo, bottom centre on a ragged knock-out patch of bare paper. Each line writes on letter by letter in about 0.35 s from its first sung syllable and holds until the next line (about 5 s). Nothing is shown during the intro, bridge or outro. The children are kept in the upper two-thirds of the frame, so the captions never cover them. The title "Rain, Rain, Go Away" is hand-lettered (Gaegu Bold) in the top-left of the page during the intro and is coloured over by the rain cloud's grey hatching at 5 s.
